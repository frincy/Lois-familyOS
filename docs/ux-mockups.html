Lois · UX Explorations · May 2026

Four directions for what Lois could feel like.

The current app is a freeform draggable canvas — maximum flexibility, minimum opinion. These are four alternative directions, each emphasizing a different facet of the Chief-of-Staff mission. They are mutually exclusive at their extremes; the strongest product is probably a hybrid. Read this alongside docs/UX-EXPLORATIONS.md for the thought process.

Open this file directly in a browser (double-click), or run npx serve docs in the repo root and visit http://localhost:3000/ux-mockups.html.

A · Briefing-First

The COS leads. One card at a time.

A real Chief of Staff doesn't make you choose what to look at. They brief you — one thing at a time. You acknowledge and advance.
Lois · Fri 7:14am 3 of 7
◌ THE LEAD
Bump Anna's middle-school registration?
Closes Friday — the only hard deadline this week. It's currently below the landscaping quote, which has no deadline. Worth moving it to the top.
↓ peek: Mom is overdue for a check-in (3 weeks)

Mission alignment

  • Eliminates the "what should I look at" decision
  • Best fit for the phone roadmap item
  • Every card is a COS judgment call — demonstrates intelligence

Trade-offs

  • Loses at-a-glance backlog visibility
  • Hard to cross-reference 3 things at once
  • Power users will feel constrained

Hero moment

  • 7am at the kitchen counter, coffee in one hand
  • The 5-minute mid-morning check-in
  • The school-pickup-line phone glance
B · Conversation-Centric

Chat is the spine. Context appears contextually.

Talking to a Chief of Staff is the interface. The chat fills most of the screen; the relevant person / loop / calendar slides in as the conversation calls for it. Nothing is "always on."

Mission alignment

  • Most "real CoS conversation" feel
  • Best fit for decide-with-me and procedural memory
  • Context is automatic — no clicking to find related info

Trade-offs

  • No at-a-glance state — you have to ask
  • Marking a loop done becomes a chat exchange
  • Requires the COS to be smart about what's relevant

Hero moment

  • Deep work moments — "I need to think this through"
  • Decision-making sessions
  • The "wow" demo for investors and first-time users
C · Day Strip

A horizontal timeline of the day. Everything anchored to when.

A household runs on time. School at 8, pickup at 3, dinner at 7. Make time the organizing dimension; let calendar, reminders, and COS check-ins live on a single timeline.

Mission alignment

  • Most natural mental model for time-pressed parents
  • Reminders / ticklers find their natural home (they ARE on a timeline)
  • Visualizes the day — doesn't make you imagine it

Trade-offs

  • Non-time items (open loops with no due date) feel awkward
  • Doesn't scale to week / month without re-design
  • Family-graph visibility is lost

Hero moment

  • The mid-day check-in: "what's now / what's next"
  • Between meetings, after pickup, in transit
  • The COS inserting a tickler on the timeline naturally
D · Family Graph

People are first-class. The household, visible.

The OS for a household should put the household at the center. Every person on screen. Every overdue cadence physically visible. Click a person → drill into their world.

Mission alignment

  • Makes the per-member family graph visible — the hardest-to-copy moat
  • Cadence + needs are first-class — overdue people physically stand out
  • New users grasp the product is about people, not tasks

Trade-offs

  • Doesn't naturally fit non-person tasks (house admin, finances)
  • Risk of feeling like "CRM for family" — wrong emotional register
  • Empty graph at first run; needs a great onboarding flow

Hero moment

  • Sunday planning — "how is everyone doing?"
  • Multigenerational households, blended families, caregivers
  • Investor demo — visually IS the moat

Recommendation

If I had to pick one to prototype first…

Phone story

Direction A (Briefing-First). Matches the morning-coffee, school-pickup, "phone in one hand" moments. Most aligned with PWA + push roadmap.

Desktop default

Direction B (Conversation-Centric). Best first-impression for new desktop users — chat-led with context that earns its place on screen.

Power-user mode

Current canvas (E). Keep as an explicit toggle for the small number of users who want drag-and-drop flexibility.

Future view modes

C (Day Strip) as a "today view" inside B. D (Family Graph) as the right-panel context when the conversation is about people.
